Why there is no report here

Your website did not show us enough about who runs the company for us to say anything useful about it. We would rather tell you that than invent something that sounds insightful.

That is not a criticism of the site. Plenty of good companies keep their team off it, and plenty of sites build that page in a way our reader cannot follow. Either way, guessing from what we could not see would produce a confident answer with nothing behind it.

The questions a website cannot answer

  • Who owns revenue today, and is that the same person who owns the product
  • Who would you call first if the next six months went better than planned
  • What decision are you putting off because nobody in the room has done it before
  • Which of your investors or advisors do you actually ring

Ten minutes on those four gets further than any amount of reading your website.

What we can still do

Connectd places up to three senior operators with early stage companies, pro bono, for three to six months. Working out which three is a conversation, and it does not depend on what your website happens to show.

Book the ten minute call

What this is, and what it is not. This report was produced by reading the company's own public website. Nobody has spoken to the team. It records what is publicly visible and, where it cannot see something, it says so rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Every gap named here carries the specific evidence it rests on and a confidence level, so any claim can be checked or dismissed in seconds.

A gap marked open question is not a finding. It means the evidence pointed that way but was not strong enough to stand behind, and the honest thing is to ask rather than assert.

Where it says we could not find something, that means exactly that. It is a statement about what your website shows us, not a claim about your company. If we have missed something that is there, the report is wrong and we would rather know.
