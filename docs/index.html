Connectd task · September 2026

Which three senior people does this startup actually need?

A lead magnet for acquiring startups, and the agentic funnel behind it. A founder enters their website. Ninety seconds later they have a read on their own leadership that is specific enough to argue with.

Hannah Tomi Ajiboye · linkedin.com/in/tomi-ajiboye

The idea

Connectd's offer to a startup is up to three senior experts, pro bono, for three to six months. So the lead magnet's output is deliberately three named senior advisory gaps, in a fixed vocabulary, each carrying the evidence it rests on.

Which means the magnet does not generate a lead for someone else to qualify. It generates the placement brief. The artefact the founder wants and the artefact the business needs are the same object.

There is a second output that earns its place. Aggregated across runs, the diagnostic tells you which advisory archetypes founders keep needing and cannot get. That is a demand forecast for exec side recruitment. The startup funnel becomes an instrument pointed at the exec funnel.

Run it yourself

Paste any early stage startup's website and get a real report in about ninety seconds. No key, no signup.

Open the tool

It runs on a capped key, so it costs you nothing and it cannot cost me much either: a separate credit limit, a pre-flight balance check so a run never dies half way, and a per visitor cap. When the budget goes it falls back to the completed runs below rather than erroring. Each run reports what it actually spent.

A diagnostic that only knows how to produce a diagnosis will produce one whether or not it should. Two of these four are the system deciding not to.

Every report renders twice, for two audiences. The founder gets the conclusion, the evidence behind it and what could not be read. Connectd gets all of that plus the working: run ids, evidence completeness, the config line that screened a company out, and every claim the adversarial pass discarded.

That split is not a setting, it is the point. Telling a founder "we also wondered whether you had no commercial leadership but decided against it" is unsettling rather than reassuring, and quoting them the config threshold that excluded them turns a useful answer into a rejection letter. Compare the refusal a founder receives with the same run as Connectd sees it.

The delivery leg is live. A report copy and an onward share to a second recipient both send, consent is logged per capture with a timestamp, and a malformed address is rejected before anything goes out. Sends are transactional rather than campaign, because a campaign attaches a List-Unsubscribe header that routes a one to one message somebody asked for into the promotions tab.

How it works

Why a closed taxonomy

21 advisory archetypes in a config file. The classifier picks from that list and may not invent one. That single constraint does two jobs. It collapses the hallucination surface, because "which of these 21 applies, and cite your evidence" is classification rather than invention, and a human can check it in seconds. And it makes the output routable, because gap names have to sit in the same vocabulary the exec bench is indexed by. Free text gap descriptions cannot be matched to a person.

It is the difference between a doctor writing a free form note and picking a diagnostic code.

Three routes in, three different openings

A founder who typed their own URL into the tool does not need to be told who sent this or that nobody asked us to look at them. They asked, ten seconds ago. A founder whose co-founder forwarded it needs enough framing that the document stands alone. A founder reached by the funding signal agent needs to be told plainly that this was unsolicited.

The first version used the cold outbound framing as the default for all three, which made the primary path open with a sentence that was not true.

RouteOpens with
Self serve"We read your public website, and nothing else." Scope and how to read it, nothing more. They typed a URL into a form ten seconds ago, so telling them nobody has spoken to their team is a disclaimer protecting the sender rather than information serving the reader.
Shared"Shared with you by someone who has already read it." Names Connectd and the offer, because the reader did not run it and may not know who we are.
Outbound"We ran a leadership gap analysis on X, and this is how we work out who might be worth introducing you to." States what was done and why it arrived.

An earlier draft of the outbound line read "nobody asked us to look at you". It was accurate and it was the wrong sentence: it puts the reader on the defensive about being looked at, before they have read anything worth reading. The version above concedes the same fact, that Connectd initiated and has not spoken to the team, while leading with the thing that might actually be useful to them.

The seams

The interesting part of a system like this is never the individual agent. It is the seams, where one agent's output becomes another's input, because that is where a plausible but wrong answer does the most damage. So the gates belong there, not sprinkled everywhere by default.

The expensive seam: evidence to named gap

Every other failure in this funnel costs a lead. This one costs credibility, in writing, in a document a founder may forward to their board, with Connectd's name on it. And it costs it with exactly the population Connectd needs to be trusted by.

GateWhat it stops
Closed taxonomyInvented gaps
Mandatory citationUnfalsifiable claims
Fewer than three allowedReaching a target for its own sake
Adversarial passContradicted and inverted claims
Leadership visibilityDiagnosing from a missing page
Confidence capThe model over rating its own certainty
Shared evidence checkTwo gaps resting on one piece of evidence
Unknowns sectionFilling silence with inference

The last three live in code, not in the prompt, and that distinction is the point. Asking a model more firmly to respect its own confidence definitions is not a control. A rule is.

Where there is deliberately no gate

Worth stating, because it is what makes the principle real rather than decorative. No gate on extraction, which is cheap and whose worst failure step 2 catches. No gate on copy variants. And no human review of an individual report when every gap rule passed, because a human in that seat at volume rubber stamps, and a rubber stamp is worse than a rule since it looks like oversight. The human gate sits instead at the point a brief would reach a senior exec, which is the first moment a wrong answer consumes a scarce person rather than a cheap API call.

The one that got through, and what it changed

The first version of this system published a false claim about a real company. It reported that Anemo Labs had a Scientific Advisory Board section with no members. Anemo Labs has three advisors: Mr Nim Arumainayagam, Prof Sergey Piletsky and Dr Todd Cowen. They are loaded into the page by JavaScript and appear nowhere in the HTML a scraper receives.

Every gate described above passed it. The claim carried a citation, it named a real page, and it survived the adversarial pass, because it was not contradicted by anything in the evidence. The evidence was simply incomplete in a way the system could not see.

A human read the report and knew it was wrong. That is the only reason it was caught, and it is the argument for the human gate rather than a decoration on it.

The root error was a reasoning mistake, not a scraping one, and it took a while to see it clearly. A heading is evidence that the thing exists. A company does not put "Scientific Advisory Board" on its team page unless it has one. The system read that heading as evidence of absence, which is close to exactly backwards.

What changed as a result

  • Pages we could not render are detected. Text to markup ratio below two per cent means most of the content was assembled by scripts. Anemo Labs measures 0.54 per cent. Ridelogix, a conventional site, measures six.
  • A heading for an unrendered section now suppresses the matching archetype outright. If the site says it has an advisory board, no advisory gap can be named, full stop.
  • Absence can no longer be a citation. A deterministic check rejects quoted evidence phrased as a negative, and drops it entirely when it comes from a page that did not render.
  • The report says what it could not read, at the top, before any finding.

These are in code, not in the prompt, because this was never a matter of asking the model more firmly.

Three more defects found the same way

All found by reading reports about real companies and disagreeing with them, rather than by testing. The fixes are more interesting than the build.

1. The qualification step did not exist

icp.yml was written first and then never wired into the pipeline, so a 29 person company with a blue chip client got a full report. A funnel that reports on everything it is handed does not have an ideal customer profile. It has a preference.

2. Customer logos cited as evidence of a sales gap

Two claims cited "Holland & Barrett, approximately 100 stores" and "BNP Paribas Portugal" as evidence the company could not sell to enterprises. Those are named enterprise customers. The evidence pointed the opposite way. The adversarial pass now runs an explicit evidence inversion check and drops both.

3. Confidence over rated on inference from absence

A gap was rated medium where the reasoning was, in effect, "there is no team page, so there is no compliance lead". That is inference from a missing page, not evidence about the company. Two fixes: a refusal gate requiring leadership visibility at all, and a mechanical confidence cap no prompt can talk its way past.

The optimisation loop

An honest description first. At thirty runs a week this is not an optimiser. It is a disciplined experiment log with an automated analyst attached, and its real value is that it makes it impossible to change three things at once and then argue about what worked.

It becomes a genuine optimiser somewhere in the low hundreds of runs a month. Claiming self improvement before that is a claim about statistics the sample size does not support.

North star: accepted placement briefs per 100 diagnostic starts. Downstream enough to be real, and the only metric that cannot be gamed by generating engagement that never becomes a placement. A thousand completions producing no accepted briefs is a failed system that looks like a successful one.

Three label sources, in rank order, because an optimiser is only as good as its ground truth:

  • Strongest: the human accept, edit or reject per gap. Skin in the game, low volume.
  • Medium: behaviour. Completed, shared, returned, booked.
  • Weakest: the founder's own yes, partly or no. Cheap, high volume, biased towards politeness. Sets direction, never decides alone.

What it reads: a 26 column run log. Every variable the loop can change and every outcome it can read has a column. Deciding the log schema is deciding what the loop is capable of learning, which is why the schema was designed before the loop was written.

What it writes: a versioned config file, and nothing else. Never the code. Never the taxonomy.

Two clocks. Fast, weekly, copy only, one variable per surface, and nothing promoted below n=30 because at low n concurrent changes are unattributable. Slow, monthly, looking for archetypes named often and rejected often, which are the taxonomy's bad entries. It opens a proposal and never merges, because the taxonomy decides what a founder gets told about their own company.

Guardrails, any breach reverts and halts: brief reject rate, gap accuracy, cost ceilings, and a minimum refusal rate. That last one exists because the loop could otherwise learn to lift bookings by naming more dramatic gaps. A system that stops refusing has learned to guess.

Distribution

An artefact with no route to founders is not a funnel.

1. Through the exec side of the marketplace

Give every senior exec on Connectd's books a personalised diagnostic link to send into their own network. Three things make this the right first route, and no other route has all three. The distributor is motivated by something better than goodwill, because a placement is the outcome they are already chasing. The relationships are warm, so it needs no verified email and no cold sender. And no competitor can copy it, because nobody else has a motivated senior exec network pointed at early stage startups.

2. The funding signal agent

A startup that just closed a round is the best qualified lead this funnel will ever see, because "get senior leadership in place fast" becomes urgent exactly when the cash lands and the board starts asking about the plan. The agent watches funding signals against the ICP and runs the diagnostic automatically on a match, so the outreach opens with a real read of their leadership rather than a template. The lead magnet becomes the outbound personalisation layer.

The verification gate is what makes that safe, and it is not theoretical. Accept only valid and deliverable. Reject catch_all, accept_all, risky, unknown, blank, and anything unrecognised, failing safe by default. A catch all domain accepts any address whether the mailbox exists or not, which is the exact condition that produces a mass hard bounce and gets a campaign pulled.

Then, in order

Accelerator and syndicate platform teams, whose job is handing portfolio companies useful free things. Connectd's own surfaces and previously unconverted startups, which is where the first thirty runs come from and therefore what unblocks the loop. Founder communities, posting rather than scraping. Paid, deliberately after the free conversion rate is known, because paid multiplies whatever rate you already have. And manual human outreach on LinkedIn, which is effective, available today, and not agentic, which is worth saying rather than dressing up.

The quality gates are also cost gates. A refusal costs about fifty times less than a full report, because it exits before the expensive model runs. The ICP screen and the refusal gate were built to stop the system saying something it could not support; at volume they also stop it spending money on companies it was never going to help. Roughly six pence a run across the four, and the cheap outcomes are the ones you want more of.

What breaks first

  • The human accept gate. The only per lead human step. Comfortable at ten a week, a full time job at a hundred, and it breaks quietly by degrading into rubber stamping. Fix: auto pass the clean cases, human review the mixed ones, random sample audit to keep the auto pass rule honest.
  • The exec bench. Demand outruns matchable supply, and no amount of funnel engineering fixes a supply problem. Naming it is what separates a marketplace plan from a lead generation plan. The archetype demand data tells exec recruitment which profiles to go and find.
  • Scrape reliability, then unit cost, then attribution once several routes are live.

What this does not claim

End to endThe diagnostic runs end to end on real companies. The routing and nurture layer is designed and has a working precedent in another stack. It is not connected here.
Knows the businessBuilt from the public offer. No access to the CRM, the bench, the real ICP or the real thresholds. Both config files exist so that is an hour of work to fix, not a rebuild.

Known limitations

  • Static fetch only. No JavaScript execution. This produced the false claim described above. The system now detects unrendered pages and refuses to build claims on absence from them, which contains the failure without removing the underlying limit: there is content on some sites it simply cannot read.
  • Run to run variance. Temperature is zero and runs still differ. On one run the adversarial pass demoted a gap and on the next it did not. A safety gate that fires probabilistically is weaker than one that fires deterministically, which is why three checks were moved into code.
  • Four runs. Everything about the loop is specification, not result.

A note on the order of work

The taxonomy and the ICP were written before the engine, and that was not sequencing for its own sake.

At a previous company I was the sole marketing hire across five product divisions, each selling a different technical product to a different industrial buyer. Five ideal customer profiles, five narratives, one person. I defined the positioning separately for each before building the engine that executed against it: five interconnected workflows handling research, drafting, image generation and publishing across five divisional sites. It cut a two day cycle to under two hours and still runs.

The part that mattered was the order. Without the ICP work first, I would have built a faster way to publish the wrong thing five times over. Same here. The taxonomy and the evidence rules are the prior work that gives the loop something worth optimising. A loop pointed at the wrong definition of a good lead just finds the wrong leads faster.
