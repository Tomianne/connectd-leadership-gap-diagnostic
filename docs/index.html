The idea

Connectd's offer to a startup is up to three senior experts, pro bono, for three to six months. So the lead magnet's output is deliberately three named advisory gaps, in a fixed vocabulary, each carrying the evidence it rests on.

Which means the lead arrives with the qualification attached. Not a name to research, but an evidenced read on what that company needs, written in the vocabulary your bench is indexed by. The artefact the founder wants and the raw material for the placement are the same object. A person still accepts or rejects each gap before it reaches an exec, and that step is built, because a draft brief and an agreed one are not the same thing.

There is a second output that earns its place. Aggregated across runs, the diagnostic tells you which advisory archetypes founders keep needing and cannot get. That is a demand forecast for exec side recruitment, so the startup funnel becomes an instrument pointed at the exec funnel.

How it works

Six steps. A human touches two of them: confirming we have the right company before anything expensive runs, and accepting or rejecting each gap before a brief reaches an exec. Everything between is unattended.

Drawn in code rather than generated: its argument is structural, and a generated image cannot be held to a structure. Every box is a function that runs.

Why a closed taxonomy

21 advisory archetypes in a config file. The classifier picks from that list and may not invent one. That single constraint does two jobs: it collapses the hallucination surface, because "which of these 21 applies, and cite your evidence" is classification rather than invention, and it makes the output routable, because free text gap descriptions cannot be matched to a person on your bench.

The seams

The interesting part of a system like this is never the individual agent. It is the seams, where one agent's output becomes another's input, because that is where a plausible but wrong answer does the most damage. So the gates belong there, not sprinkled everywhere.

Every other failure in this funnel costs a lead. One costs credibility, in writing, in a document a founder may forward to their board. So all eight gates sit at that single seam, evidence to named gap.

The three in code are the point. Asking a model more firmly to respect its own confidence definitions is not a control. A rule is.

Where there is deliberately no gate

Naming this is what makes the principle real rather than decorative. No gate on extraction, which is cheap and whose worst failure the confirmation step catches. None on copy variants. And no human review of an individual report when every gap rule passed, because a human in that seat at volume rubber stamps, and a rubber stamp is worse than a rule since it looks like oversight.

How it fails, and what catches it

The question worth asking about a system like this is not whether it is clever. It is whether you would put your name on what it tells a founder.

An early version told a startup its Scientific Advisory Board section was empty. The board has three members. They load into the page by JavaScript and appear nowhere in the HTML a scraper receives.

Every automated gate passed it. The claim carried a real citation, named a real page, and survived the adversarial pass because nothing in the evidence contradicted it. It failed plausibly, which is the failure mode that matters: an error announces itself, a confident wrong answer does not.

A person read it and knew it was wrong. That is the argument for keeping a human gate rather than a decoration on one, and it is why the accept or reject step before a brief reaches an exec is built rather than described.

The fix was a reasoning one, not a scraping one. A heading is evidence the thing exists. A company does not put "Scientific Advisory Board" on its team page unless it has one. Now: unrendered pages are detected by their text to markup ratio, a heading for an unrendered section suppresses the matching archetype, and absence can never be a citation. All three in code.

The optimisation loop

An honest description first. At thirty runs a week this is not an optimiser. It is a disciplined experiment log with an automated analyst attached, and its value is that it makes it impossible to change three things at once and then argue about what worked.

North star: accepted placement briefs per 100 diagnostic starts. The only metric that cannot be gamed by generating engagement that never becomes a placement.

Three label sources, in rank order, because an optimiser is only as good as its ground truth: the human accept or reject per gap, which is strongest and now collected; behaviour, which is medium; and the founder's own yes, partly or no, which is cheapest, highest volume and politeness-biased, so it sets direction and never decides alone.

Two clocks. Fast, weekly, copy only, one variable per surface, nothing promoted below n=30 because at low n concurrent changes are unattributable. Slow, monthly, looking for archetypes named often and rejected often, which are the taxonomy's bad entries. It opens a proposal and never merges, because the taxonomy decides what a founder gets told about their own company.

Guardrails, any breach reverts and halts: brief reject rate, gap accuracy, cost ceilings, and a minimum refusal rate. That last one exists because the loop could otherwise learn to lift bookings by naming more dramatic gaps. A system that stops refusing has learned to guess.

Distribution

An artefact with no route to founders is not a funnel.

1. Through the exec side of the marketplace

Give every senior exec on your books a personalised diagnostic link for their own network. No other route has all three of these: the distributor is motivated by the placement they are already chasing, the relationships are warm so it needs no verified email, and no competitor can copy it.

2. The funding signal agent

A startup that just closed a round is the best qualified lead this funnel will ever see, because "get senior leadership in place fast" becomes urgent exactly when the cash lands. The agent watches funding signals against the ICP and runs the diagnostic automatically on a match, so the outreach opens with a real read of their leadership rather than a template. The lead magnet becomes the outbound personalisation layer.

A verification gate makes that safe: accept only valid and deliverable, reject everything else including catch_all, which accepts any address whether the mailbox exists or not and is the exact condition that produces a mass hard bounce.

Then

  • Accelerator and syndicate platform teams, whose job is handing portfolio companies useful free things.
  • Your own surfaces and previously unconverted startups, which is where the first thirty runs come from and therefore what unblocks the loop.
  • Paid, after the free conversion rate is known.
  • Manual outreach on LinkedIn, which is effective and not agentic, worth saying rather than dressing up.
A screened-out company is a lead for the other side. Piclo has eighteen staff and a CFO; DrDoctor raised a ten million Series B. Neither wants three pro bono advisors. But the senior operators inside them are exactly who your exec side recruits. Piclo's CFO is not a customer, she is supply. The screen changes which side of the business the lead belongs to rather than discarding it.

What breaks first

The human accept gate. Comfortable at ten a week, a full time job at a hundred, and it breaks quietly by degrading into rubber stamping. Then the exec bench, where demand outruns matchable supply. No funnel engineering fixes a supply problem, and naming it is what separates a marketplace plan from a lead generation plan.

Known limitations

  • Static fetch only. No JavaScript execution, which is what produced the failure above. Contained now, but there is content on some sites this cannot read.
  • A filter only one side speaks is a silent reject. The ICP once targeted a stage value the classifier could never emit, so every Series A company was screened out with a confident, correctly worded explanation and nothing looked broken. A startup check now refuses to run on a vocabulary mismatch.
  • Run to run variance, and a narrow target band. Temperature is zero and runs still differ, which is why three gates sit in code. And a rich public footprint correlates with being further along, so thin sites get refused, rich sites get screened out, and the profile sits between them.

A note on the order of work

The taxonomy and the ICP were written before the engine, and not for its own sake.

At a previous company I was the sole marketing hire across five product divisions, each selling a different technical product to a different industrial buyer. I defined the positioning separately for each before building the engine that executed against it: five interconnected workflows running research, drafting, image generation and publishing across five divisional sites. Two day cycle to under two hours, still running.

The order was what mattered. Without the ICP work first I would have built a faster way to publish the wrong thing five times over. A loop pointed at the wrong definition of a good lead just finds the wrong leads faster.
